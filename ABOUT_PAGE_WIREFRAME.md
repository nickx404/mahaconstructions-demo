## Responsive layout notes

- Keep the desktop header in one row with the logo on the left, page links across the center, and the contact action at the right. On mobile, use the logo and a clearly labelled menu control; expose all navigation links in the menu.
- The hero remains typographic at every width. Desktop uses a deliberately offset composition with the main title occupying a broad field; mobile stacks the title and supporting lines in a readable left-aligned column. Keep decorative letterforms and linework behind the text.
- The founder section places portrait and quote side by side on desktop. On mobile, stack the portrait first, then the quote, attribution, and short biography.
- The story timeline runs horizontally across desktop and vertically down mobile. Show only milestones supplied and approved by the company.
- The number strip uses a single horizontal row on wide screens and a vertically separated sequence on narrow screens; each figure remains paired with its label.
- The “Why Maha Constructions” section pairs approved photography and editorial statements on desktop. Stack them on mobile without reducing statement text to dense cards.
- The four principles form a two-column typographic arrangement on desktop and a single-column list on mobile.
- Keep the closing statement high contrast. Stack its two calls to action on small screens if needed to preserve comfortable button widths.
- The footer changes from multiple columns on desktop to grouped, full-width blocks on mobile.
- Use generous chapter spacing on desktop and consistent, smaller section padding on mobile. Avoid horizontal overflow from oversized background lettering or linework.

## Design description

### Composition and visual language

The page should read like an architectural editorial: measured, spacious, and grounded in the company’s history. Its hero establishes the visual language rather than trying to summarize every section. Use an off-white or warm-white field with a very faint technical grid. Place a large, low-opacity “M” or “MC” partly beyond the viewport as background structure, then layer a few fine construction lines, a restrained arc, and small coordinate-style marks around it. Keep all such elements behind the copy, with enough contrast separation that the headline stays immediately legible.

Use section markers such as “02 / THE FOUNDER” and “03 / OUR STORY” as small navigational annotations, not prominent decorative badges. A fine rule can connect a marker to its section heading or continue into a timeline. Align details to a consistent underlying grid while allowing the major title, founder quote, timeline, and metrics to break that grid deliberately. This creates asymmetric balance without making the page feel scattered.

Reserve the deep-green field for the closing statement and footer. Elsewhere, favor warm white, near-black or deep-green text, quiet concrete-gray rules, and small accents from the approved Maha palette. Avoid decorative imagery: the actual founder and company project photography can be inserted later in the clearly marked slots.

### Typography

Use Geist Variable, bundled with the About page, for the oversized “ABOUT / MAHA / CONSTRUCTIONS” title and major statements. Its variable weights support a broad, architectural display while keeping body copy and navigation readable. Keep display lines broad and few rather than compressing the words into a narrow column. Use lighter weights for the founder quotation and stronger weights for compact section markers and metric labels. A restrained monospaced face may be added only for small coordinate details if those annotations need a more technical drawing quality.

Create hierarchy through scale, weight, line length, and whitespace: oversized hero title; large, editorial section headings; a prominent founder quotation; medium-sized milestone titles and principle names; compact metadata and captions. Use sentence case or uppercase intentionally and consistently. Keep body copy at a comfortable reading size and line length. Avoid ultra-light small text and over-tight tracking that would harm legibility.

### Architectural details and spacing

The grid, rules, arc, timeline, cropped monogram, and measured alignments should suggest a drawing set or building plan, not imitate a literal blueprint. Keep strokes thin and pale, use only a few per composition, and leave open space around the page title and founder quote. Let each section feel like a distinct chapter with generous vertical separation; use tighter spacing only within related groups such as an attribution, metric and label, or principle and description.
